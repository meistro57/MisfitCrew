### Analysis of Hardware Glitches

A "Hardware Glitch" in a belief system is a logical paradox, a functional failure, or a point where the system's rules cannot be consistently applied. The RAW DATA here contains several such glitches.

**Glitch 1: The Paradox of Cyclical, Deity-Specific Time vs. Ancestral Lineage.**
- **The Glitch:** The first CLAIM establishes a cyclical time with days dedicated to *specific divine figures* (Deity-specific calendrics). The second and third CLAIMS state that deceased ancestors have continued influence and are worshipped in the home. If time is strictly cyclical and tied to pantheons, when do ancestors get their "day"?
- **The Failure:** The system attempts to merge a top-down, cosmic, pantheonic calendar with a bottom-up, familial, lineage-based practice. An ancestor is not a general deity. When the "Day of God X" arrives, which is more powerful: the cosmic rule of the cycle, or the immediate, domestic presence of the ancestor? A practitioner would face a **scheduling conflict** of cosmic proportions. Does the ancestor's influence trump the deity's day, or is the ancestor only to be honored during "off-peak" cosmic hours? This creates a ritual impossibility if both are mandatory.

**Glitch 2: The Spatial Limit of Domestic Ritual Space vs. Ancestral Influence.**
- **The Glitch:** The system demands that ancestor worship be "ritually maintained within the physical dwelling" (Domestic ritual space) and that "designated markings within the home serve as focal points." The first CLAIM, however, implies a belief in an ancestor's *continued influence and interest* in all aspects of their living relatives' lives.
- **The Failure:** This creates a **jurisdictional problem**. If an ancestor's power is spatially tethered to the home (they are only a force *inside* the dwelling, where the markings are), how can they have "continued influence" when a family member leaves the home to work, trade, or war? The glitch is a spatial paradox: the ancestor is both a powerful, interested agent in the world of the living, yet their worship is locked inside a specific box (the house). The system cannot logically explain how the ancestor's power extends beyond the threshold of the home without either 1) a second set of rules for "mobile ancestor power," or 2) the implication that the ancestor is actually too weak to leave, contradicting their general influence.

**Glitch 3: The Identity Crisis of the Focal Point.**
- **The Glitch:** The system requires "designated markings... as focal points for honoring the spirits of the dead." The ECHOES mention "Syncretic religious practices" and "Animism."
- **The Failure:** If the marking is a truly *functional* focal point (an actual home for the spirit), what happens if the marking is damaged, destroyed, or the house is renovated? Has the family committed a hostile act against the ancestor? If the marking is merely *symbolic* (a representation), then the ancestor is presumably elsewhere, but the ritual requires a specific physical space. This creates an **epistemological glitch**: the system cannot be coherent about whether the spirit *is* the marking, *lives in* the marking, or *is summoned to* the marking. A syncretic, animistic system would likely fluctuate between all three, causing ritual anguish and confusion.

### Analysis of Ontological Shock
