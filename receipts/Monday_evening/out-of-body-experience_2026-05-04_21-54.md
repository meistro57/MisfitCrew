## The Big Idea
An out-of-body experience (OBE) is when you feel like your “you-ness”—your consciousness—pops out of your body and can float around or see things from outside. People from ancient India to modern-day meditators all describe the same thing: your mind can leave your body while you're still alive, and it’s not just a dream.

## The Receipts

**Source 1: Dolores Cannon, *The Custodians* (1998)**
A woman tells how she suddenly felt a high-pitched sound, then her awareness drifted out of her body. She saw herself from above and felt physical sensations that weren’t coming from her actual body. The idea is that you can be awake, feel real touch and fear, while your consciousness is clearly separate from your flesh-and-blood self. This is the same OBE pattern reported worldwide.

**Source 2: Bashar (channeled by Darryl Anka, 1980s–present)**
Bashar says astral projection isn’t about “leaving” the body. Instead, it’s expanding your sphere of awareness until your physical body is just a small part of what you’re aware of. So you don’t go anywhere—you just realize you were never trapped inside your head. That’s the same core idea: consciousness isn’t stuck in the body.

**Source 3: Seth Speaks (Jane Roberts, 1972)**
Seth explains that death is just a longer version of the same “pulsation” that happens during sleep or meditation. Consciousness shifts to a different frequency and stops focusing on physical reality. The body is built by consciousness, not the other way around. So even in life, you can temporarily shift that focus and have an OBE. Same message.

**Source 4: Upanishads (~800 BCE – 200 CE)**
An ancient Indian sage named Yajnavalkya describes the Self as the “witness” that sees everything but can never be seen. He says the soul can exit the body through a tiny opening and travel beyond the physical world. The idea that the real you is an observer separate from your body—same as every OBE account.

**Source 5: ROOT ACCESS (2025)**
A modern guy starts as a skeptic, tries sound-frequency meditations, and has an OBE. He feels his consciousness lift out of his body while his ears hear a specific tone. Now he’s convinced: brainwave states can trigger the same separation described for thousands of years. Same fundamental experience, just with headphones.

## Why This Is Wild
These people lived centuries apart, in different continents, with no internet or books to copy from. An ancient Hindu philosopher, a 1970s psychic channeler, a 1990s hypnotherapist, and a 2020s sound‑frequency nerd all independently described the exact same thing: your mind can leave your body. That’s not a coincidence—it’s either a real phenomenon or the most persistent hallucination in history.

## The Weird Part (Hardware Glitch)
Here’s the paradox: if your consciousness can really leave, why does it feel like you’re still attached by a “silver cord” or an invisible tether? And why do some people see real physical things during their OBE (like what’s on a high shelf they couldn’t see before) while others see only symbolic landscapes? The experience says “I’m really out,” but the rules don’t match ordinary physics. It’s like your brain is running an app that both works and glitches at the same time.

## What It Means For You
You don’t have to believe in ghosts or spaceships. Just try this: tonight, as you’re falling asleep, tell yourself softly, “I am not my body. I am the one watching my body.” Don’t force anything. The next time you have a dream where you’re floating, pay attention—you might already be having OBEs and just calling them dreams. The point isn’t to prove it to anyone else; it’s to realize that the “you” inside your head might be a lot bigger than your skull.
