## The Big Idea
Reality isn't a solid, fixed place you just walk into. Multiple sources from different times and cultures all say the same thing: you're not just *in* reality — you're *building* it with your mind.

## The Receipts

**Source 1 (Chapter 15)**  
This text describes reality as having multiple layers — a shared physical world plus hidden, non-material dimensions. It says there's an agreed-upon version of reality we all experience, but that's not the whole story.

**Source 2 (Session 661, May 7, 1973)**

**Source 3 (Session 640, February 14, 1973)**

## Why This Is Wild
These sources span ancient Eastern philosophy (Advaita Vedanta), early 20th-century esoteric thought (Theosophy), 1970s channeled sessions, and modern mindfulness movements. They had zero contact with each other. Yet they all converge on the same idea: mind comes first, reality follows.

## The Weird Part (Hardware Glitch)
Here's the paradox: if reality is a projection of your mind, then the claim "reality is a projection of your mind" is *also* a projection of your mind. That means it can't be objectively true — it's just another thought. So the whole idea collapses into a loop: you can't prove it without using the thing it's trying to prove. Also, if everyone builds their own reality, how do we all agree on the same mountain, the same language, the same traffic light? That shouldn't work unless something else is going on.

## What It Means For You
You don't have to accept this as literal truth to get something useful out of it. Try this: for one day, act like your thoughts are shaping your experience. If you feel stuck, ask: "What am I *making* this situation?" You'll notice your mood shifts your perception instantly. That's not woo — it's just paying attention. The real takeaway: stop waiting for reality to change you. You're the one changing it.
