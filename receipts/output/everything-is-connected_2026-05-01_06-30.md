## The Big Idea
Everything in the universe — you, a rock, a star, your thoughts — is connected at a basic level. It’s not a metaphor. It’s like they’re all part of the same invisible web, sharing information and influence across time and space.

## The Receipts
- **Advaita Vedanta (ancient India)**: This Hindu philosophy says there’s only one real thing — called Brahman — and everything else, including you, is just a mask it wears. The idea: deep down, we’re all the same stuff, not separate.
- **Buddhist Indra’s Net (ancient India/China)**: A story about a net hanging over the universe with a jewel at every knot. Each jewel reflects all the others, so every part contains the whole. The idea: each thing mirrors every other thing — total connection.
- **Hermeticism (ancient Egypt/Greece)**: The saying “as above, so below” means that what happens in the stars also happens in a grain of sand. The idea: all levels of reality — big and small — are linked and mirror each other.
- **Systems Theory (20th century science)**: This says everything — from your body to a forest to the economy — is a system where parts affect each other. The idea: you can’t change one thing without shaking the whole network.
- **Quantum Entanglement (modern physics)**: When two particles get linked, changing one instantly changes the other, no matter how far apart. Einstein called it “spooky action at a distance.” The idea: the universe isn’t as separate as it looks — connection can be instant and weird.

## Why This Is Wild
These sources come from different continents, centuries, and cultures — India, Greece, modern labs — with zero contact. Yet they all landed on the same picture: a universe where nothing is truly alone. That’s like a kid in Egypt, a monk in India, and a scientist in a lab all drawing the same invisible monster without talking.

## The Weird Part (Hardware Glitch)
Here’s the problem: If everything is truly connected, how do we explain obvious separation? You can’t feel what I feel. A rock doesn’t think. Random stuff happens — like a sneeze or a quantum jump — that seems disconnected. The idea struggles to explain why the world looks so fragmented if it’s secretly one thing. It’s like saying the ocean is one wave — it’s true deep down, but surface-level, it’s not.

## What It Means For You
This isn’t just a fun fact. It means your actions ripple outward in ways you can’t see. Being kind to a stranger? That might echo through the web. Hurting the environment? You’re hurting yourself. The practical takeaway is simple: treat everything — people, animals, the planet — like it’s part of you, because in this view, it kinda is. You don’t have to believe it; just act like it’s true and see what happens.
