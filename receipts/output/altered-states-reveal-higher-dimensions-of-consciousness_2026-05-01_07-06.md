## The Big Idea
When you change how your brain works—through sleep, meditation, or other methods—you can see parts of reality that are normally hidden. Many different people across history say this is a real thing, not just imagination.

## The Receipts
- **Seth / Jane Roberts (1970s):** An entity named Seth, channeled by Jane Roberts, claimed that sleep lets you bypass your brain's filters to perceive higher dimensions. He said "neurological structures act as filters or barriers to multidimensional perception." This is the same idea: your brain is a bottleneck, and altered states open it up.

- **Shamans and Mystics (ancient to present):** For thousands of years, shamans in Siberia, the Amazon, and elsewhere used drums, plants, or fasting to enter altered states. They all reported contacting spirits or seeing other layers of reality. The core claim matches: non-ordinary states reveal a bigger, non-physical world.

- **Vedanta and Platonism (ancient India and Greece):** Hindu texts like the Upanishads (800 BCE) and Plato's Allegory of the Cave (380 BCE) both said the physical world is just a shadow or illusion. They argued that deeper meditation or philosophical awakening lets you perceive the true, higher-dimensional reality. Same idea: change your state, see more.

- **Aldous Huxley (1950s):** In *The Doors of Perception*, Huxley said the brain normally acts as a "reducing valve" that filters out most of reality. Psychedelics temporarily disable this filter, letting in a flood of higher-dimensional information. He called the hidden reality "Mind at Large." Again: altered states = access to higher dimensions.

## Why This Is Wild
These sources span 3,000 years, multiple continents, and completely different cultures—shamans in the jungle, philosophers in ancient Greece, a psychic in 1970s New York, and a British novelist on LSD. None of them had contact with each other. Yet they all independently described the same weird idea: that our normal brain is a kind of cage, and breaking out of it shows a bigger, more real world.

## The Weird Part (Hardware Glitch)
Here's the problem: if altered states reveal objective higher dimensions, how do you prove that? The experience feels real, but it could just be your brain hallucinating. The sources themselves admit this—Seth says "symbolism is linked to states of consciousness," meaning the experiences are wrapped in personal images. Plus, the idea that "non-ordinary states let you perceive more" is circular: you define "total reality" as whatever you perceive, so any altered perception automatically counts. We have no way to tell if it's a genuine window or just a brain bug.

## What It Means For You
You don't need to take drugs or join a cult. Start simple: pay attention to the moments between sleep and waking, or try a short meditation where you just watch your thoughts. People who do this often report strange feelings—like seeing the world as more alive or feeling connected to something bigger. Whether that's real or just your brain glitching, it's a free experiment you can run yourself. Don't believe it; try it and see what happens.
