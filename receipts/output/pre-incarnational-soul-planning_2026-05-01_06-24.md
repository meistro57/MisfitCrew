## The Big Idea
Before you were born, your soul sat down and picked out your life like you’re choosing a character in a video game—your family, your health, even your biggest struggles. Then you forgot you did it.

## The Receipts

**Source 1: Jane Roberts, Seth Speaks (1972)**
Seth, a non-physical entity speaking through a woman in upstate New York, said that your physical body isn’t an accident. Your soul deliberately picked it before you were born, like ordering a custom outfit.

**Source 2: Plato’s Myth of Er (circa 380 BCE)**
In ancient Greece, Plato told a story about a soldier who died and saw souls picking their next lives from a set of options. They chose wealth, poverty, or even being an animal. Then they drank from the River of Forgetfulness and were born, remembering nothing.

**Source 3: Seth/Jane Roberts, The Nature of Personal Reality (1974)**
Seth claimed that you design your life’s limitations and tragedies on purpose—like a workout plan for your soul. You even pick your parents and your illnesses, all to balance out strengths and weaknesses from other lives happening at the same time.

**Source 4: Dolores Cannon, Conversations with Nostradamus Vol. 1 (1989)**
A hypnotist named Dolores Cannon found that some souls choose to be born just to help others. Some do it for the adventure. Others have to come back or they’ll get stuck and stop growing forever.

**Source 5: The Ra Contact (1981)**
Ra, a collective of beings speaking through a channel, said that before you’re born, you make “pre-incarnation agreements” with your family and friends. You all decide ahead of time what lessons you’ll teach each other—like actors agreeing on a script before the play starts.

**Source 6: Jane Roberts, The Education of Oversoul Seven (1973)**
A fictional soul named Seven remembers choosing to be born into a specific family in a dusty, historical period. Why? Because he knew he was too logical and needed to learn how to feel.

## Why This Is Wild
These sources come from ancient Greece, a psychic in New York, a channeler in Kentucky, and a hypnotist in Arkansas. They span 2,400 years and had zero contact with each other. Yet they all describe the same bizarre idea: you pick your life before you live it, then promptly forget. That’s not coincidence—that’s a pattern.

## The Weird Part (Hardware Glitch)
If you’re a smart soul picking your life with full knowledge, why do you have to forget? That’s like studying for a test and then taking it blindfolded. And if all your lives happen at once (as Seth claims), there’s no “before” to plan anything—you’re already in all of them. The system breaks: how can you choose a life if you’re already living it?

## What It Means For You
You don’t have to believe this. But if it’s even slightly true, then your biggest pains aren’t random—they’re stuff your soul signed up for. That doesn’t make them easy. It makes them meaningful. Next time you’re in a mess, instead of asking “Why me?” try asking “What did my soul come here to learn?” That shift alone changes everything.
