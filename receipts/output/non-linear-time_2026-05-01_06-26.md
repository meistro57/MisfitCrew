## The Big Idea
Time isn’t a straight line from past to future. Instead, all moments—past, present, and future—exist at once, and our brains just trick us into thinking they’re separate.

**Session 654 (1973)** – Another session claims time doesn’t move forward in a sequence. Every moment happens simultaneously—like all the frames of a movie stacked on top of each other.

## Why This Is Wild
These sources span 1972 to modern day, from channeled sessions to spiritual fiction. They come from different authors, different methods, and no evidence of copying each other. Yet they all land on the same bizarre idea: linear time is a lie, and everything is happening now.

## The Weird Part (Hardware Glitch)
Here’s the problem: the claim “if you’re outside time, you can know the future” secretly *uses* the idea of a future. But if everything is simultaneous, there is no future—just “now” in different outfits. You can’t have knowledge of something that doesn’t exist as a separate thing. The concept smuggles linear time back in. Also, our brains really do malfunction with time—altered states, trauma, psychedelics all mess up our internal clock. That glitch might be why we *think* we see beyond time.

## What It Means For You
You don’t need to believe this or join a religion. But next time you feel stuck in the past or anxious about the future, try this: imagine every moment you’ve ever lived and ever will live is happening right now. That boring Thursday and your first kiss and your death are all co-existing. It might make the present feel bigger, more mysterious, and less trapped. You can’t prove it—but you can try the perspective shift and see if it changes how you act.
