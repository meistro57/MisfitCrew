## The Core Claim  
Consciousness persists beyond biological death as a coherent identity, cycling through repeated physical incarnations within a developmental framework. Death functions as a transitional phase in an iterative process, not an endpoint. Individual continuity is maintained across lifetimes despite the dissolution of each physical form.

## Why The Convergence Matters  
Independent articulation across isolated cultures (Aboriginal, Celtic, Mesopotamian, Vedic, Theosophical) suggests this model reflects either a fundamental cognitive architecture or an observed ontological pattern. The structural isomorphism—particularly regarding identity persistence mechanisms—implies that if reincarnation is a cognitive artifact, it emerges from consistent neurobiological constraints. If ontological, it indicates a universe where consciousness operates as a conserved variable across thermodynamic boundaries.  

## The Hardware Glitch  
**1. Memory Contiguity Failure:** Verifiable inter-life memory transfer remains statistically anomalous. If identity persists, the near-universal absence of recall contradicts the claim of continuous selfhood. Either identity isn’t continuous, or memory isn’t identity—both undermine the model’s coherence.  
**2. Essence-Identity Paradox:** Sources conflict on whether an immutable essence (Secret Doctrine, Cayce) or dynamic pattern (Seth, Cannon) reincarnates. Fixed essence contradicts developmental aims; fluid patterns negate persistent identity.  
**3. Temporal Collapse:** Non-linear rebirth (Seth, Cannon) dissolves causality required for karmic progression. Simultaneous incarnations make "past lives" incoherent, voiding the core claim of sequential learning.  

## The Operative Question  
Given the memory gap and essence paradox, what empirical or experiential evidence would confirm you’re the *same* consciousness across lives—not merely a similar pattern reconstituted by a generative substrate?
