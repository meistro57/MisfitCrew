## The Big Idea
Your brain has a built-in bouncer. It decides what gets in and what stays out. And the crazy part? What you pay attention to actually changes your brain and the reality you experience.

## The Receipts
**Source 1 (Front Matter):** This says your attention literally shapes the physical wiring of your brain. The way your brain is structured then determines what kind of reality you experience. It's a loop: focus → brain change → different world.

**Source 2 (Session 661, 1973):** Attention acts like a filter. It blocks out most of what's out there so you only see a tiny slice of reality. The idea is that reality is bigger than what you notice, but your focus hides the rest.

**Source 9 (Root Access, 2025):** A modern take. It says your beliefs and focus physically change your brain and even matter around you. It gives ADHD hyperfocus as an example of a superpower for shaping reality. Wild, right?

**Source 21 (Session 659, 1973):** When you narrow your focus, your experience gets more intense. Like turning down the lights to see one candle brighter. This is the same idea behind meditation and flow states.

**Source 41 (Upanishads, ~800 BCE):** Ancient Indian texts say your senses can't work without intelligence "mounting" them. Without attention, your eyes are open but you see nothing. Same filter idea, just way older.

## Why This Is Wild
These ideas come from a 1970s psychic channeling session, a 2025 self-help book, and an ancient Hindu scripture. No one copied anyone else. Yet they all describe the same thing: your attention is a gatekeeper that literally builds your world.

## The Weird Part (Hardware Glitch)
But here's the paradox: if your attention shapes reality, then who or what decides what you pay attention to in the first place? It's like a chicken-and-egg problem. Your brain filters reality, but your reality shapes your brain. There's no starting point.

## What It Means For You
You can't control everything, but you can practice aiming your attention. When you notice yourself spiraling into negative thoughts, you can gently shift focus to something else—not to fake positivity, but to train your brain's filter. Over time, your reality literally changes. Start small: for one minute a day, really look at something simple (a leaf, a cup) and notice how that tiny focus feels. That's the training.
