These sources span Babylon (1000 BC), Egypt (200 AD), India (500 BC), medieval alchemy, 19th-century occultism, and 20th-century psychology. None of these people were copying each other. Yet they all ended up using Moses as a symbol for hidden power, secret knowledge, or cosmic transformation. That’s a lot of independent minds landing on the same idea.

## The Weird Part (Hardware Glitch)

The sources contradict each other: some say Moses is historical, others say he’s a myth, others say he’s a title. The glitch is that all these things can’t be *literally* true at once. But they’re all *psychologically* true — the human mind keeps coming back to the same image.

## What It Means For You  
You don’t have to pick just one version of Moses. Instead, you can treat him like a shared dream across cultures. When you see the same story showing up in Babylon, Egypt, India, and modern psychology, it’s a clue that something real is being pointed at — maybe a universal human experience of meeting a higher authority, breaking old rules, and discovering hidden knowledge. The next time you hear “Moses,” ask yourself: which Moses are they talking about? The prophet? The magician? The symbol? The answer changes everything.
